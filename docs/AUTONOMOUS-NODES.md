# Autonomous nodes: every node acts from its type and its orders

Status: **approved 2026-11-06 (section 7); stages 1 to 8 built** (bd epic murmur-y85j; section 8
says what stage 6, the migration, did and found; section 9 the guards and the air-to-air
redesign; section 10 SITL vehicles taking damage). What else remains is in section 9.

The owner, 2026-11-06: "I fundamentally [want] the nodes to act correctly in all usages. we
create resources that are combined in situations and they act correctly based on their coding.
I know the situations were made before weapons, so make sure to arm all nodes correctly. so the
scenario is instructing it to attack at a certain point, not hot wiring the scenario to see an
attack. [...] node need to be able to act in a self contained way through an interface if this
is to scale".

## 0. In short

- Our side already works this way: a platform's catalog entry (`sim/platforms.toml`, `loadout`,
  rules 24 to 28) says what it carries, an order says what to do, the engine judges it. The
  other side does not: an attack is an `[[act]]` (a second, an author, a victim) and a contact
  fires whatever it carries, or nothing, when the file says so.
- Proposed: a **contact catalog** (`sim/contacts.toml`) in the same shape as ours, armed and
  unarmed variants as distinct kinds; **behaviours** each contact runs itself (transit, hold,
  orbit, track, attack, break off, flee); **fire control** inside the node (weapon choice, range
  band, interval, rounds, what it does when it cannot fire); **orders** in the contacts file or
  through the contacts process's own API, so a situation says "attack HUNTER 1 from 3:20" and the
  node decides when and with what it fires.
- The C2 side does not change: a node's shot reaches the bridge as the same truth (`acts`,
  `weapons`, `impacts`), sensed by the same physics, reported to the engine as the same
  `ActReport`. **No rules version.** Section 2.7 checks this claim place by place; two
  by-construction shortcuts remain, unchanged.
- The old path stays for what must stay frozen (engine scenarios, the ident evaluation, the
  headless golden) and is refused for live situations by a load check (section 3).
- Seven stages, four of which can run as parallel agents (section 5); seven decisions for the
  owner, each with a default (section 6).

## 2. The model

### 2.1 A contact catalog

`sim/contacts.toml`, one table per kind, read by `crates/murmur-contacts` (a new `catalog.rs`).
It replaces the `KINDS` table (`scenario.rs:109-127`) and the per-contact `weapon = {...}`:

```toml
[armed_fast_boat]
name = "Fast inshore attack craft (ATGM team, HMG)"
looks_like = "small_fast_boat"   # what our sensors and the C2 see; absent: itself
domain = "surface"
signature = 0.35                 # the legacy model's multiplier, as KINDS today
emits = false
turn_dps = 12
cruise_mps = 15
max_mps = 25                     # [R]
sensors = { eye_m = 4000, radar_m = 0 }   # what the node itself sees (section 2.4) [R]
armed = true
loadout = [
  { name = "ATGM-class missile", kind = "missile", count = 4, min_range_m = 100, max_range_m = 5000,
    speed_mps = 250, profile = "direct", guidance = "saclos", warhead = { class = "tandem_heat", mass_kg = 5 },
    targets = ["surface", "ground"], effects = { ... }, reload_s = 30, sense = "atgm" },   # [R] every figure
  { name = "12.7 mm HMG", kind = "gun", count = 500, burst_rounds = 6, rate_rpm = 525, burst_interval_s = 5,
    min_range_m = 0, max_range_m = 1800, speed_mps = 850, ..., sense = "gun" },
]
```

- **Schema.** A loadout entry is `catalog::WeaponFacts` (`crates/murmur-decide/src/catalog.rs:326`)
  plus two truth-side keys: `reload_s` (seconds between shots of a missile or rocket team) and
  `sense` (which of the bridge's weapon signatures our sensors see: `atgm` today). The contacts
  crate does not depend on the decision engine, so it gets its own struct that reads the same
  TOML; one test (in `crates/murmur-headless`, which depends on both, as `KIND_ALIASES` is
  checked today) parses every catalog loadout with both and compares the shared fields.
- **Figures.** A new `docs/research/opponent-weapons.md` in the style of docs/research/weapons.md:
  every figure `[n]` or `[E]` with its basis, public sources only. The rule our sensors follow
  (decision W3, "never tune a sensor to make a situation work") applies here: **never author a
  node's figures to make a situation work**.
- **Variants.** Where real life differs, the variants are distinct kinds (`small_fast_boat` and
  `armed_fast_boat`). `looks_like` keeps the C2 side unchanged: the truth message's `kind` is the
  look-alike (the bridge's signatures, tracker and identification read only that), and a new
  truth-only `variant` field (omitted when equal to `kind`) names the catalog kind for the
  evaluation log. Decision D3.
- **A scenario's contact** names its `kind` and keeps its motion, AIS, signature overrides and
  timing as today. `weapon = {...}` on a contact is refused once the tutorial is migrated.

### 2.2 Behaviours

Each contact runs one order at a time (its file's motion is order zero). Names follow our task
vocabulary where the meaning is the same (`crates/murmur-decide/src/intent.rs:30-50`); the type
is not shared, because ours is judged by the engine and theirs is truth.

| order | parameters | the node does |
|---|---|---|
| `transit` | `route`, `speed_mps?` | once along the route, then holds (today's `transit`; a `maneuver` is this) |
| `hold` | `at?` | stops where it is or at `at` (a boat stations against the current) |
| `orbit` | `center`, `radius_m`, `speed_mps?` | circles (today's `loiter`) |
| `track` | `target`, `standoff_m` | keeps that distance from its target as it moves (shadowing) |
| `attack` | `target`, `weapon?`, `rounds?`, `until_s?`, `stay?` | fire control below; `stay = true` fires only from where it is |
| `break_off` | | ceases fire, keeps its motion |
| `flee` | `from?`, `speed_mps?` | away from `from` (or from the nearest threat it perceives) at its top speed, along its way out |
| `resume` | | back to the file's motion |

`target` is `{ vehicle = <uid> }` (one of ours) or `{ contact = "<id>" }`. Steering for `track`,
`attack` and `flee` keeps to the node's domain on the same land/water grid the scenarios already
name (`[sensing] terrain`, e.g. `sim/headless/terrain/golden-gate.grid.gz`): a straight leg over
the wrong surface is not taken; the node holds and logs `no_way` (no planner on the truth side in
this pass). A kind may give neutrals a reaction (`on_fire_nearby = "flee" | "continue"`), so a
fishing boat near a strike leaves on its own.

### 2.3 Fire control (inside the node, every tick)

1. **Target perceived?** (section 2.4). No: close on its last perceived position; unseen for
   `lost_s` (60 s [E]), the order ends `target_lost` and the node holds.
2. **Weapon**: the named one, else the first of the loadout (catalog order) meant for the target's
   domain, with rounds left, not out of action (`Mover::disarmed`, `motion.rs:49`, a firepower
   kill). The same rule our engine uses (`loadout::choose`). None: the order ends `no_weapon`.
3. **Range band** (slant, `min_range_m` to `max_range_m`). Inside: fire when ready. Beyond: close at
   `max_mps` toward the target's lead point, unless `stay`. Inside the minimum: open out to it, as
   rules 28's firing position does for ours.
4. **Ready**: `reload_s` since its last missile, `burst_interval_s` since its last burst.
5. **A shot**: a missile, rocket or torpedo is one `ActTruth` with a weapon flight (today's
   `Armory`, `weapon.rs:90`, generalised to three dimensions: `fly_m` is capped at 100 m today,
   `scenario.rs:335`, so nothing can be fired at an aircraft); a gun's bursts are **one act per
   firing episode** (bursts within 30 s of the last against the same victim belong to it: one
   `ActReport` for the engine, as one engagement is one nomination for us), each burst a strike
   drawn after its time of flight; a `self` weapon (one-way drone, explosive boat) is the node
   steering into its target, its act committed at the strike.
6. **Rounds** count down per shot (bursts by rounds). `rounds` on the order caps a salvo. Out of
   rounds: the order ends `out_of_rounds` and the node follows its kind's `on_empty` (default
   `flee` along its way in).
7. **Damage**: mobility lost, it fires from where it stopped; weapons out, it holds fire;
   destroyed, it is gone (today's damage states, `motion.rs:42-49`).
8. **Every decision is logged** in the truth message (`node_events`: order taken, closing, fired,
   held and why, ended and why), evaluation-only, absent when empty. The bridge never reads it.

Act ids are `<contact>-<n>` in firing order, so the damage draws (`act:<id>`) stay stable across
runs; the existing `effect:<intent>` streams of our strikes are untouched.

### 2.4 Perception

A node attacks what it can see, not what the file names. Default (decision D2): each tick the
truth message asks for ours near each node with an order that needs a target (`look: [{ node,
lat, lon, reach_m }]`, replacing `aim_at`); the bridge's answer `ours` (today
`tracks/mod.rs:2005`, `ours_for` at `:551`) carries those within reach. The node keeps those
inside its kind's `sensors` reach and above the radio horizon for both heights. Shortcuts,
stated: positions are **what our vehicles report to our C2** (today's shortcut), a destroyed one's
last report lingers until the bridge drops it, no terrain masking on the truth side, and a
node's own sensors are a reach and a horizon, not the physical model.

### 2.5 The order interface

- **In the contacts file**: a top-level `[[order]]` list, the replacement for `[[act]]`, on the
  contacts' clock as acts are today (`hostile-act-engagement.toml:7-11` says why that clock runs a
  few seconds behind the situation's):

  ```toml
  [[order]]
  at_s = 200
  node = "fastboat-raider"
  do = "attack"
  target = { vehicle = 1 }
  weapon = "ATGM-class missile"   # optional
  label = "attacks the sensor ship (HUNTER 1)"
  ```
- **Live, through the contacts process's own API** (decision D1): `murmur-contacts` listens on
  `MURMUR_CONTACTS_PORT` (a new port in each block: default stack, situations, eval), every
  request checked against `MURMUR_SIM_TOKEN` as `POST /api/sim/contacts` is.
  `POST /orders` (the same JSON as a file entry, `at_s` absent meaning now): `{ id, accepted }`
  or `{ refused: why }` (unknown node, weapon not carried, unknown target, node destroyed).
  `GET /orders/{id}`: its state and why it ended. `GET /nodes`: each node's own state (position,
  order, rounds left, damage) and **what it perceives**, never the truth of ours.
- **From a situation's timeline**: a new step kind `red` (`eval/situation.py` `STEP_KEYS`: `node`,
  `do` and the order's keys), posted by `eval/situation_run.py` at its `at_s`, director-only by
  default (it is truth). Fixed scripts belong in the contacts file; a timeline step is for orders
  that depend on the run.
- **Headless**: `crates/murmur-headless/src/sim/contacts.rs` runs the contacts code in process; file
  orders work as live, a `red` step is applied in process at its second.
- **A future red-side agent**: an MCP server over the same API with its own token
  (`MURMUR_RED_TOKEN`), tools `get_nodes` and `order_node`, never on the bridge, so no agent of
  ours can reach it and the red agent sees only its nodes' perception. Not in this plan's
  stages; the API is shaped for it.
- **Shared vocabulary**: names and parameter shapes yes (`transit` with `route`, `orbit` with
  `center` `[lon, lat]` and `radius_m`, `hold`, `track`); code no.

### 2.6 Effects on ours

Unchanged machinery: a strike with figures is drawn by the damage model against the victim's kind
(`damage::ours_kind`, `damage.rs:172`: the Sea Hunter reads as a fishing vessel), written to
`sim/run/damage-<http port>.jsonl` (`MURMUR_DAMAGE_FILE`), acted out by the stand-ins
(`murmur_standins::damage`: unpowered, silent, or a `DAMAGE` STATUSTEXT every 30 s) and reported to
the C2 by the vehicle itself (rules 25). **SITL vehicles act it out too** since stage 8
(section 10, `sim/sitl_damage.py`, a thread of `sim/swarm.py`): mobility through SITL's own
`SIM_ENGINE_FAIL` on the kind's propulsion outputs, the same `DAMAGE` report over the vehicle's own
link, a destroyed vehicle's process stopped (silent). A sailboat and an antenna tracker only
report. The headless simulator runs orders and
opponent weapons with the contacts process's own step since stage 5 (`crates/murmur-contacts/src/
step.rs`, both call it; docs/HEADLESS.md 16.6): ours a strike hurts take it in the episode's
fleet, and a timeline's `red` step goes to the API's own intake (`api::Desk`) in process.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

1. **Where orders enter.** Default: **the contacts process's own API** (section 2.5). The red side
   gets its own interface, the blue bridge never relays or holds a red order, and a red agent later
   plugs into it with its own token. The alternative, a bridge endpoint passed on in the simulation
   channel's answer (as effects and mines are), reuses one token but puts red orders through our
   C2's process.
2. **How nodes perceive ours.** Default: **ours as our C2 hears them, within the kind's reach and
   horizon** (section 2.4). Cheap, honest about reach, its shortcuts stated. Alternatives: full
   physical sensing on the truth side (large; the payload managers and draws are the bridge's), or
   omniscience (the hot-wiring this plan removes).
3. **Armed variants look like their civilian twins.** Default: **yes** (`looks_like`): the C2 sees
   a fast boat, never "an armed fast boat", until it acts; the tracker, identification and engine
   are unchanged and no rules version is needed. Showing the variant at the identify level would
   be truer for a mounted weapon in plain view, but changes the engine's inputs (new kinds in
   `TrackFacts` and the weapon check's figures).
4. **Opponent damage on, and seeds.** Default: **real figures in every migrated situation**, so
   HUNTER 1 can be damaged or lost and the brief says so; **no seed is picked to spare ours**. The
   seeds already picked so our own weapon's default run shows a kill (H8) stay: they choose among
   the model's draws, documented and tested, and pinning nothing. The stricter alternative drops
   them too, and the default runs then often show a miss.
5. **The tutorial's training aids.** Default: **keep the pinned kill (11-03) and add one aid,
   both under `[training]` and refused outside the tutorial**: strikes on HUNTER 1 are drawn and
   recorded but not applied (`spared`), so a new person is not taught a lost sensor ship before the
   chain. The boat gets its real magazine (ATGM, then HMG episodes), not 13 free missiles. The
   alternative, fully honest, needs a coach card for a damaged or lost HUNTER 1.
6. **Headless and the shared hostile-act-engagement file.** Default: **build headless weapons
   (stage 5, murmur-mmxk) before migrating it**, so `hostile-act-loitering-munition` keeps its
   headless smoke episode. The alternative freezes an abstract copy for the headless test, which
   keeps the old path in a live situation's family.
7. **Saturation's shooter.** Default: **an armed drone kind with a light stand-off missile [R]
   among the recon swarm**, firing from where it holds, so the reserve's engagement and its 7 km
   clearance are as now. The alternative, a quadcopter that drops a munition, must fly over the USV
   2 km east, which changes the engagement's geometry and the load test's story.

## 10. Stage 8 as built: SITL damage (2026-11-06, bd murmur-y85j.8)

Our SITL vehicles now act out the damage file as the stand-ins do (section 2.6), as far as SITL
allows. `sim/sitl_damage.py` runs as a thread of `sim/swarm.py`, beside the SITL processes it
starts (so `scripts/up.sh` is unchanged). It follows the file the stand-ins read
(`MURMUR_DAMAGE_FILE`, else `sim/run/damage-<http port>.jsonl`), skips records already there when
the fleet started and not written since (a previous run's: the contacts process empties the file
when it starts, which is after the fleet), and acts per vehicle:

| damage | the SITL vehicle | how |
|---|---|---|
| mobility | loses its propulsion; its autopilot flies on unaware, as one whose motor has died | `SIM_ENGINE_MUL` 0, then `SIM_ENGINE_FAIL` set to the kind's propulsion outputs (below), by PARAM_SET until echoed. SITL scales those outputs before its physics model reads them (`libraries/AP_HAL_SITL/SITL_State.cpp`, the `engine_fail` loop), so the vehicle comes down or to a stop by forces its IMU feels. And the report |
| firepower | moves as before | the report |
| destroyed | silent: neither steps, sends nor answers | its SITL process stopped (SIGSTOP). Its sockets stay open, so the link emulator and the bridge meet silence (the bridge's `lost_link` alert), never an error. A killed process would refuse the emulator's datagrams and drop the tidewater driver's TCP link, whose failed reconnect the driver does not catch (and `up.sh` stops the stack when any of its processes exits). `swarm.py`'s shutdown sends CONT after TERM so it exits |

The report is the stand-in's words (`DAMAGE mobility: propulsion lost`, `DAMAGE firepower: weapons
out of action`, severity CRITICAL), at once and every 30 s while the vehicle can talk. It enters on
the vehicle's SERIAL2 (TCP 5763 + 10 x instance; the tidewater driver has SERIAL1) from the
vehicle's own system id as its onboard computer (component 191: ArduPilot drops a message bearing
its own system and component as a loop), and ArduPilot's MAVLink routing forwards it, as any
broadcast, on every link where it has heard a ground station: the bridge's among them. The bridge
reads a STATUSTEXT from any component of a vehicle's system, so its `damage` fact, `damage` event
and `damaged` alert follow as from a stand-in. The PARAM_SETs make ArduPilot echo `SIM_ENGINE_*`
PARAM_VALUEs on every link, as it echoes the tidewater driver's `SIM_WIND_*` and `SIM_TIDE_*`; the
bridge reads none of them. No rules version, no engine change, no firmware patch.

The propulsion outputs, from each frame's ArduPilot defaults and SITL model (a failed output goes
to 1000 us, ArduRover's to 1500, its neutral; `PROPULSION` in `sim/sitl_damage.py`):

| SITL kind (catalog entries) | outputs failed | what follows |
|---|---|---|
| plane (`puma`, `switchblade`) | SERVO3, the throttle | glides down |
| vtol (`fvr90`) | SERVO3 and SERVO5-8, the lift motors | glides or falls |
| copter (`x10`, `fpv`) | SERVO1-4 | falls |
| blimp (`lta`) | SERVO1-4, the fins (SIM_Blimp's thrust comes from fin speed: held still they push nothing) | drifts on the wind |
| boat (`magura`, `corsair`) | SERVO3, the throttle, and SERVO1, the steering: our boats steer by their thrust (waterjet or outboard), where SITL's rudder would still turn the boat on what way it has left | coasts to a stop, then drifts on the current (tidewater's; without it the motorboat defaults' `SIM_TIDE_SPEED` 0.5 m/s from the north) |
| ugv (`ugv_logistics`, `ugv_armed`, `ugv_mines`, `ugv_recon`) | SERVO1 and SERVO3, left and right throttle | stops |
| sub (`bluerov`) | SERVO1-6, the thrusters: 1000 us is outside the band SIM_Submarine takes as thrust | rises on its slight positive buoyancy |
| sailboat (`saildrone`) | none | sails on: reports only |
| tracker (`tracker`) | none | does not travel: reports only |

**What SITL cannot do, and the gaps.** (1) A sailboat's sail draws on the wind, and no SIM
parameter takes that away: a failed mainsail servo holds the sail at a fixed angle, which still
sails. A saildrone hit for mobility reports it and sails on; closing it needs a SITL patch (a
sail failure parameter) and every firmware rebuilt. (2) A sub's thrusters held at 1000 us draw
SITL's full simulated current, 15 A each (SIM_Submarine counts current from the output, not the
thrust), so its reported current reads 90 A and its voltage sags about 3 V after a mobility hit;
SITL's battery has unlimited capacity by default, so nothing runs down. SITL maps a failed output
around 1500 us for ArduRover only, so no `SIM_ENGINE_MUL` gives a sub zero thrust at neutral
current. (3) The autopilot's own logic stays: a copter that has fallen may sit armed in GUIDED at
full commanded throttle where a stand-in rests unpowered, and ArduCopter adds its own "Potential
Thrust Loss" text; the C2 has the damage report either way. (4) Destroyed freezes the vehicle
where it was hit, in the air if it was flying. A destroyed stand-in does the same, and nothing in the stack reads a
SITL vehicle but through its link (the bridge, the link emulator, the truth side through the
bridge), so a fall nobody hears would change nothing. (5) `--keep-eeprom` keeps a damaged
vehicle's `SIM_ENGINE_FAIL` into the next run (PARAM_SET saves). (6) Found on the way, not fixed
here: the stand-in engine reads the damage file from its start when it starts, and nothing
empties the file in a stack without a contacts process, so a previous run's records on the same
HTTP port (a `make goldengate` with `CONTACTS=` that hurt ours, then one without) damage this
run's stand-ins of the same uids; with a contacts process it is a race the contacts process
usually wins. The SITL watcher skips such records.

**Checked live** (`sim/tests/sitl_damage_live.py`: the fleet started by `swarm.py`, the bridge
talking to the vehicles directly, the records appended by hand; telemetry from each vehicle's
SERIAL1 and the bridge's view kept in `logs/stage8/` of the worktree):

- **Air** (a Puma at 150 m and 20 m/s in TAKEOFF mode, an X10 holding 40 m in GUIDED; mobility on
  both at 77.9 s). The Puma's autopilot went to full throttle (SERVO3 2000 us) and the aircraft
  came down: 145.8 m at 80 s, 100.2 m at 100 s, 42.5 m at 130 s, 13.6 m at 145 s, near the stall
  (8 to 12 m/s). The X10's motors went to about 1,940 us and it fell: 24.5 m at 80.2 s, SITL's
  "Hit ground at 16.1 m/s" at 81.8 s, ArduCopter's own "Potential Thrust Loss" twice. Both EKFs
  stayed healthy throughout (flags 831, velocity and position variances 0.06 or less). Each
  reported `DAMAGE mobility: propulsion lost` at 78.2 s (0.3 s after the record) and again at
  108.2 and 138.3 s; the bridge logged a `vehicle.event` `damage` for each and raised both
  `damaged` alerts. Firepower on the X10 at 138.4 s: both reports from 139.2 s, the alert now
  "propulsion lost and weapons out of action". The Puma destroyed at 146.4 s: its last telemetry at
  147.1 s, then the bridge's `lost_link` alert ("no telemetry since ..."), nothing logged as an
  error. At the stop every SITL port was free, the stopped Puma's included. Repeated with the
  script as committed: the Puma from 150 to 27 m in the minute after the hit, the X10 on the
  ground 3.8 s after it ("Hit ground at 16.1 m/s"), the same reports, alerts and silence.
- **Surface** (a recon UGV at 4.0 m/s and a Corsair at 8.0 m/s in GUIDED, a BlueROV holding
  12 m down in GUIDED; mobility on all three at 66.4 s). The UGV stopped (0.13 m/s at 68.1 s,
  0.03 by 76 s) with its autopilot at full throttle on both tracks. The Corsair coasted to 0.08
  m/s by 76 s at full throttle, then drifted astern at 0.47 m/s on the motorboat defaults' current
  (no tidewater in the check). The BlueROV rose on its buoyancy at 0.10 m/s (11.98 m down at
  68.1 s, 6.35 m at 126 s, 1.66 m at 174 s) while its autopilot drove its vertical thrusters at
  1,900 us; its reported current went from 5.6 to 90 A and its voltage from 12.4 to 9.6 V (gap
  2; no battery failsafe fired). All three reported at 67.2 s and every 30 s after, with the
  bridge's `damage` events and alerts; firepower on the Corsair joined its report; the UGV
  destroyed at 135.0 s went silent at once (last telemetry 135.1 s, then `lost_link`). EKFs
  healthy throughout; every SITL port free at the stop. A first run with only the boat's throttle
  failed turned it on its remaining way under full rudder (heading 1 to 277 degrees) before it
  drifted, which is why the steering goes too.

Not run live: the quadplane and the blimp (their outputs read from SIM_QuadPlane's motor offset
and SIM_Blimp's fin model), the sailboat and the tracker (reports only, the firepower path). The
watcher's mapping and its file handling have unit tests (`eval/tests/test_sitl_damage.py`).
