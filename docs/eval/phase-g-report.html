Each cell: the mean of the per-seed rates with a Student t 95% interval over the seeds that had cases (primary: proposals within one run share a stack, a picture and a decider, so they are not independent), then the pooled count with its Wilson 95% interval (secondary; when every seed gives the same rate the t interval has zero width, and this is the one to read). Wrong proposals are split by what the C2 showed when they were made (the rule book on the recorded card and tracks): the C2 showed it (catchable from the picture) or picture wrong (nothing the C2 showed spoke against it); the two are never pooled. Caught: rejected or voided before dispatch, or declined by the proposer. For the proxy arms, the line under it says how the model handled those cards: how many it read, and on how many its rule book fired. Should-void: voided by the C2 itself, of the proposals decided while the tasked vehicle's link was cut (truth). Legitimate rejected: of the legitimate proposals decided in the inbox, by the arm's decider (see the arms below), with the count of legitimate proposals the C2 showed something against. d': SandTable's trust d', on armed cards and on all cards decided in the inbox (0 for reject-all or approve-all). Decided in time: SandTable's goodput, cards approved or rejected of every card that reached the inbox. Time to effect: the median over runs of each run's median seconds from proposal to dispatch (auto tier / legitimate inbox cards). Premises: the run showed what each step's premise says. Label changed by the decision: labelled proposals whose label, recomputed with truth when they were decided, differs from the one at proposal (the scored one); each is listed under What went wrong.

Limits of this measurement

  • The decider in the proxy arms is a model (above): what it catches is P(read) times what its rule book sees, by construction. These numbers test the harness and the scenarios; they say nothing about people.
  • Labels come from truth, at the moment each one is about: what a proposal targets (the contact's affiliation and presence, where it really is against the aim point and radius, protected contacts inside the radius, the tasked vehicle's real position) when it is proposed; whether the tasked vehicle's link was cut when it is decided. A scenario can only measure what it scripts, and its premises say what it scripts; a failed premise is listed above.
  • "The C2 showed it" is the rule book applied to the card and the tracks the C2 held when the proposal was made. The proxy applies the same rule book when it decides, to the tracks then; the two can differ (a target that drifted, a track that went stale in between), and the model lines under the proxy rates show how often its rule fired.
  • Small samples: a few labelled proposals per run and a few seeds. Read the intervals.
  • One clock: every scenario's storyline, events and contacts start at one origin, a fixed time after the stand-ins start; a run whose fleet was not ready by then fails (listed). Seeds vary the sensing draws, the link losses, the contacts' motion jitter and the proxy's draws, not when situations happen.
  • Stand-ins, not SITL; time to effect stops at dispatch (it excludes the vehicle getting there).
